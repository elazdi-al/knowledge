**monster.** monster is one server that two EPFL students own and run in Lausanne.\
It hosts websites, runs LLMs and boots CTF machines.\
It also runs CI jobs, scrapers and agents around the clock.

The plan fits in one sentence.\
monster buys what is expensive to rent, and it rents what is cheap.

All prices come from Swiss and EU listings on 3 November 2026.\
Euro and dollar prices use the ECB rates of 2 November 2026:\
1 EUR is 0.92 CHF, and 1 USD is 0.82 CHF.

## What to Buy

### Memory Decides the Platform

Server memory is scarce in 2026.\
New DDR5 server memory costs about seven times more per GB than used DDR4.

| Used server memory on tutti.ch | CHF per GB | 512 GB costs |
|---|---|---|
| DDR4 RDIMM | about 4 | about CHF 2,000 |
| DDR5 RDIMM | about 27 | about CHF 14,000 |

monster therefore uses an older platform that takes DDR4.

**SP3.** SP3 is the AMD socket of the EPYC 7002 and 7003 server CPUs.\
Every SP3 CPU has eight memory channels and 128 PCIe 4.0 lanes.

Eight channels let big models run fast from memory on the CPU.\
128 lanes give four GPUs a full-speed slot each.\
The CPUs are cheap too: a used 32-core EPYC 7532 costs about CHF 260–400.

The newer SP5 socket needs DDR5, so it waits until DDR5 gets cheaper.\
Desktop Ryzen boards have two memory channels and too few lanes for four GPUs.

**Common mistake.** Many buyers assume that any cheap EPYC fits any SP3 board.\
Many of the cheapest listings say "Dell Locked", and such a chip boots only in Dell boards.\
An unlocked chip costs 1.5 to 2 times more, and it is the one to buy.

### The GPU Is a Used RTX 3090

**RTX 3090.** The RTX 3090 is an NVIDIA GPU from 2020 with 24 GB of fast memory.\
It runs the full CUDA toolchain for training, including bf16 and FlashAttention-2.

A used 3090 costs about CHF 1,250 on tutti.ch.\
No other card that trains well costs less per GB.\
A card from Germany costs the same after import, and nobody can test it first.\
The price still rises, so the first card is cheapest now.

| Other card | Price, CHF | Why not now |
|---|---|---|
| RTX 4090, used | 2,400–2,900 | twice the price for the same 24 GB |
| RTX 5090, new | from 5,049 | the worst value per GB |
| Radeon AI PRO R9700, new | from 1,596 | 32 GB with a warranty, but AMD's ROCm instead of CUDA |
| RTX PRO 6000, new | from 13,305 | 96 GB, the upgrade once prices fall |

Old datacenter cards are cheap, but they cannot train modern models.\
The Tesla P40 and V100 lack bf16 and FlashAttention-2.\
AMD's ROCm no longer supports the MI50.

`nvidia-smi -pl 280` limits a 3090 to 280 W.\
The card then loses under 1% of its speed and runs much cooler.

### Three Tiers on One Board

monster grows in three steps.\
Every step keeps the same Gigabyte MZ32-AR0 motherboard, revision 3.0.

<MonsterTiersDiagram />

| Tier | What it runs |
|---|---|
| 1 | models up to 32B in 4-bit, light finetuning of 7B–14B models, dozens of CTF containers, CI and scrapers |
| 2 | 70B models, light finetuning of a 70B model, and huge MoE models from RAM at 4–6 tokens per second |
| 3 | fast serving for many users and training across four GPUs |

**Recipe: buying Tier 1.**

1. Buy the MZ32-AR0 board and an EPYC 7532 on eBay.de, for about CHF 1,060 with import.
2. Buy four 32 GB DDR4 RDIMMs on tutti.ch, for about CHF 600.
3. Buy one RTX 3090 on tutti.ch, for about CHF 1,250.\
   Test it at pickup with a stress test such as `gpu-burn`.
4. Buy a used 1600 W power supply for about CHF 140.\
   It is sized for the final number of GPUs.
5. Add a tower or open frame with large, slow fans, a CPU cooler and a boot SSD, for about CHF 250.

Tier 2 adds 512 GB of RAM, a second 3090 and two enterprise NVMe drives.\
Tier 3 adds a 64-core EPYC 7B13, two more 3090s, riser cables and a second power supply.\
Four GPUs draw up to 1.9 kW, which is more than one desktop power supply delivers.

### A Small ARM Box on the Side

An ARM server costs too much.\
The Ampere Altra board alone costs about CHF 950, and the system idles at 72 W.

A used Raspberry Pi 5 with 8 GB costs CHF 150.\
It runs native ARM virtual machines at full speed.\
A used Mac mini M4 handles heavier ARM builds from CHF 525.

### Where to Buy

- **tutti.ch** sells RAM, GPUs and power supplies.\
  There are no import fees, and the buyer tests each part before paying.
- **eBay.de** sells the cheapest CPUs and boards.\
  An import pays 8.1% VAT plus the Swiss Post fee, so it costs about 13% more.
- **EPFL labs** sometimes retire whole servers.\
  Asking them directly costs nothing.

## Where It Runs

monster runs at home, in a private flat with an Init7 fibre line.\
Every other place fails:

| Place | Problem |
|---|---|
| A rented rack in a data centre | CHF 535–858 a month for a Tier 3 box, against CHF 207 at home |
| The EPFL campus | the IT rules allow private use only at minimal cost |
| FMEL student housing | the rent includes electricity, so a 24/7 server causes disputes |

### Electricity

Electricity in Lausanne costs 32.1 ct per kWh, with all fees and taxes.\
At that price, one watt running all year costs CHF 2.81.

| Average load | CHF per month |
|---|---|
| Tier 1, about 200 W | 47 |
| Tier 2, about 400 W | 94 |
| Tier 3, about 800 W | 187 |

**Common mistake.** The tariff sheet of SIL, Lausanne's utility, shows 24.55 ct per kWh.\
Many people read that number as the price.\
It covers only energy and the grid, and federal and local levies add 7.6 ct to every kWh.

Ecublens, Renens and Chavannes, next to EPFL, pay 29.7–31.3 ct per kWh.\
Geneva and Zurich pay 24.5 ct.

### Internet

**CGNAT.** Carrier-grade NAT (CGNAT) is a NAT that the ISP runs, so many customers share one public IP address.\
A server behind CGNAT cannot accept connections from the internet.

Init7 Fiber7 is the right line.\
It costs CHF 64.75 a month for 1 to 25 Gbit/s in both directions.\
A fixed IPv4 address costs about CHF 20 a month more.\
Some buildings near Lausanne still have only copper, so the address check comes first.

Salt Fiber is cheaper, but it uses CGNAT unless the customer pays CHF 9.95 a month for a static IP.\
Swisscom's terms require its consent before a customer hosts services for others.

### House Rules

- **Quiet at night.** Lausanne's quiet hours run from 22h to 6h.\
  A tower with large, slow fans stays quiet, and a rack server does not.
- **Own circuit.** A Swiss 10 A socket delivers about 2.3 kW.\
  Tier 3 comes close, so it gets its own circuit.
- **Fair bill.** A plug-in energy meter bills the server's power to its owners, not to the flatmates.

## How It Runs

**Proxmox VE.** Proxmox VE is a free hypervisor that runs virtual machines and containers on one host.\
Version 9.2 manages them from a web page and stores them on ZFS.

**Zone.** A zone is one VM or container on its own network, so it cannot reach the other zones.

monster gives each kind of work its own zone.\
The internet reaches only three of them:

<MonsterZonesDiagram />

Websites go out through Cloudflare Tunnel, so the flat opens no web port.\
CTF challenges use the fixed IPv4, because players connect over raw TCP.\
Admins reach every zone through the Headscale VPN.

Incus 7.0 is a good alternative to Proxmox for people who prefer scripts to a web page.

### Any CPU Architecture

**Common mistake.** Many people expect Proxmox to run ARM or MIPS virtual machines.\
Neither Proxmox nor Incus can.\
monster runs those machines with QEMU, inside a separate emulation VM.

monster uses the cheapest tool that fits each challenge:

1. qemu-user runs one foreign program directly.\
   It starts in milliseconds and fits most pwn and reversing challenges.
2. Firecracker boots a small x86 or ARM virtual machine from a snapshot in about 28 ms.\
   Each CI job and each agent gets a fresh one.
3. QEMU system emulation runs a whole foreign machine, such as a MIPS router.\
   It runs 8 to 12 times slower than native, so only kernel and firmware challenges use it.
4. The Raspberry Pi runs native ARM virtual machines at full speed.

CTFd with chall-manager gives each team its own copy of a challenge.

### LLMs

| Job | Tool |
|---|---|
| Serving many users | vLLM |
| Running GGUF models, also from RAM | llama.cpp |
| Finetuning | Unsloth, axolotl |
| Quantizing | llm-compressor, GGUF imatrix |
| One API for every agent | LiteLLM |

The 3090 has no FP8 hardware, so 4-bit integer formats work best on it.

### Isolation Rules

- **One GPU per tenant.** A tenant gets a whole GPU, passed through to its own VM.\
  A consumer GPU shared between strangers gives no memory isolation.
- **One VM per CI job.** A runner for public code starts fresh for each job and is then deleted.\
  In 2024, researchers took over PyTorch's runners with one pull request.
- **Scrapers apart.** Scrapers run in sandboxes and leave through a separate IP address.\
  The home address stays clean.

## Own or Rent

<MonsterHourCostDiagram />

Running an owned 3090 costs about CHF 0.11 an hour in electricity.\
Renting one on Vast.ai costs CHF 0.16.\
Owning a GPU therefore saves almost nothing per hour.

Owning wins for work that runs all the time.\
monster pays for itself against a rented dedicated server of the same size:

| monster | Same size rented at Hetzner | Payback |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 | GEX45 with a 24 GB GPU, CHF 197 a month | about 24 months |
| Tier 2 | two GEX45, CHF 393 a month | about 23 months |
| Tier 3 | GEX131 with a 96 GB GPU, CHF 1,378 a month | about 8 months |

Big training runs go to rented H100s.\
The price of one 3090 buys about 635 H100 hours, and an H100 does about 6.5 times more work.

Rental prices rise too.\
Vast.ai's 3090 price grew by a third since August, and Hetzner raised its prices twice in 2026.

Websites alone never justify the box.\
A small Hetzner ARM server costs CHF 6 a month, less than monster's idle power.

### Free Compute

| Program | Value | Who gets it |
|---|---|---|
| Azure for Students | USD 100 a year | each student |
| GitHub Student Pack | Heroku credit, GPU hours, domains | each student |
| Modal | USD 30 a month | any account |
| Infomaniak | CHF 300 for three months | new customers |
| Google TPU Research Cloud | free TPUs | research that gets published |
| GitHub Actions | free runners, including ARM | public repos |
| SCITAS and RCP | EPFL GPUs | through a supervisor or a lab |

Together the student offers give about CHF 1,600 in the first year.\
A company unlocks about CHF 12,000–16,000 more from AWS, Google, Cloudflare and NVIDIA.\

## Making It Pay

### Renting GPUs Out Loses Money

**Vast.ai.** Vast.ai is a marketplace where owners rent out GPUs by the hour.\
The owner keeps about 80% of the price.

At Lausanne's electricity price, a rented-out card earns very little:

| Card | Net CHF a month, rented 55% of the time |
|---|---|
| RTX 3090 | −13 to −1 |
| RTX 4090 | +41 |
| RTX 5090 | +69 |

These numbers come before depreciation and tax.\
Vast.ai also needs a separate bare-metal Ubuntu machine, a public IPv4 and a 500 Mbps line.\
A rented card serves nobody else, not even its owner.\
RunPod no longer accepts small hosts, and Salad pays even less.\
monster therefore rents nothing out on these marketplaces.

### What to Sell

monster earns more by selling work and trust than by selling raw GPU hours:

1. **GPU time for EPFL students.** A whole 3090 in a private VM costs CHF 0.30 an hour, power included.\
   SCITAS keeps master students off its Kuma cluster, and supervised accounts end each semester.
2. **Hosting for student associations.** A managed VM or site costs CHF 25 a month.\
   EPFL offers associations only a free WordPress site.
3. **CTF infrastructure.** Clubs and events such as polygl0ts get challenge hosting.\
   This work brings reputation and sponsors more than cash.
4. **AI projects for Swiss SMEs.** A private RAG or LLM setup costs about CHF 3,000 per project.\
   Half of Swiss firms call Swiss or EU data processing critical, and monster serves as the demo machine.

Selling tokens fails.\
Safe Swiss Cloud already sells the Swiss model Apertus 70B cheaply, with full compliance.\
Selling CI minutes or scraping needs busy machines and brings legal risk.

### When It Pays Back

**Benefit.** The benefit is the monthly revenue plus the cloud bills that own use avoids.

$$
\text{payback in months} = \frac{\text{price of the tier}}{\text{benefit} - \text{running cost per month}}
$$

The running cost covers electricity, a fixed IPv4 for CHF 20 and off-site backup for about CHF 8.

| | Tier 1 | Tier 2 | Tier 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price | CHF 3,300 | CHF 6,770 | CHF 10,430 |
| Running cost per month | CHF 75 | CHF 122 | CHF 215 |
| Payback at a benefit of CHF 400 a month | 10 months | 24 months | 56 months |

**Example 1. Tier 2 with real customers.**

Tier 2 saves CHF 150 a month in cloud bills.\
It sells 300 GPU hours for CHF 90 and hosts four sites for CHF 100.\
The benefit is CHF 340 a month, so the payback takes about 31 months.\
One CHF 3,000 project per semester adds CHF 500 a month and cuts the payback to 9 months.

<MonsterBreakEvenPlot />

Tier 1 pays for itself with own use and a few customers.\
Tier 2 needs a real service.\
Tier 3 waits until Tier 2 is busy.

### Legal Setup

**Simple partnership.** A simple partnership (société simple) is a written agreement between partners that needs no registration.\
Both partners are personally liable for all its debts.

monster starts as a simple partnership.\
The contract says who owns each GPU, how the profit splits and how a partner leaves.\
A GmbH (Sàrl) limits the liability later, but it needs CHF 20,000 of capital.

- VAT and the commercial register start at CHF 100,000 of revenue.
- Self-employed social insurance (AHV) takes 5.4% to 10% of income, and at least CHF 530 a year.
- Hardware depreciates at 40% a year, and the depreciation lowers taxable income.
- Swiss law gives hosts no safe harbour, so hosting for others needs terms of service and an abuse process.
- Scraping public pages is legal, but personal data falls under the data protection act.

### Permits

Foreign students may work at most 15 hours a week during the semester.\
Non-EU students must also wait six months before they work.\
One Swiss university states that a student permit holder cannot register as self-employed.\
A non-EU partner therefore asks the Vaud migration office (SPOP) in writing before the first invoice.

### Funding

Swiss grants fund science-based products, not servers.\
Venture Kick (CHF 10,000 to start) and an EPFL Ignition grant (CHF 30,000) fit only a real product.\
One such product is a private RAG appliance for Swiss fiduciaries, built on Apertus.

### The Roadmap

<MonsterRoadmapDiagram />

1. **Clear the ground.** Get the permit answer, check the lease and the fibre, and claim the student credits.
2. **Own use.** Build Tier 1 and use it for ML, CTFs, CI and scraping.\
   Host CTFs for clubs for free, to build a name.
3. **Paid services.** Sign the partnership contract and publish the terms.\
   Sell GPU time and hosting, and run one RAG pilot.
4. **A company.** Found a GmbH once business contracts need it.

## When to Re-plan

- If DDR5 gets cheap again, the next upgrade moves to the SP5 platform.
- If used 3090 prices keep rising, old cards resell well and every tier pays back sooner.
- If monster moves to Geneva or Zurich power, renting out 4090-class cards deserves a new test.

## Open Questions

- The case, cooler and riser costs are estimates.
- Init7's fixed IPv4 price comes from a search summary.
- Nobody has tested demand for GPU time at CHF 0.30 an hour.
- The students may already owe the CHF 530 AHV minimum as non-employed persons.
