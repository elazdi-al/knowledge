A note doesn't have to be all words. When the thing you want to talk
about is a piece of interaction, the cleanest demonstration is the
interaction itself, embedded in the paragraph that mentions it.

## A drawer

Tap the button. The panel slides up from the bottom of the viewport,
the rest of the page dims, and you can drag the handle down to dismiss
or tap outside. Closing it fires a toast: small, transient, in the
bottom corner.

What's interesting about a drawer isn't the slide animation. It's the
two affordances that come with it for free: **drag to dismiss** and
**dim the background**. Both reinforce a single idea: *this is
temporary*. The page below is still the real surface; the drawer is
borrowed time.

## When a drawer beats a modal

| pattern  | feels like        | best for                            |
| -------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| modal    | a hard stop       | confirmations, destructive choices  |
| drawer   | a side-glance     | quick edits, brief reads, settings  |
| popover  | a footnote        | secondary info, small actions       |
| sheet    | a context switch  | flows that briefly need full focus  |

A drawer is for things you want to **glance at**, not commit to. The
toast that follows it is the same idea inverted: a result you want to
**acknowledge** without acting on.

## A note about libraries

The two components above are off-the-shelf. Vaul for the drawer,
Sonner for the toasts. Neither tries to invent a new pattern. They
just do the boring parts (focus traps, gesture handling, queue
management) so the rest of the page can stay quiet.
